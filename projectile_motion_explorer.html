Theory, Kinematics & Rigorous Derivations

Click on any core formula card to expand its step-by-step mathematical derivation.

1. Initial Velocity Resolution

Expand

Resolving initial launch speed $v_0$ at angle $\theta$ with horizontal:

$v_x = v_0 \cos\theta$   |   $v_{y0} = v_0 \sin\theta$

Derivation: Using right-angled triangle trigonometry on velocity vector components:

• Adjacent side along horizontal gives $v_x = v_0 \cos\theta$. Since $a_x = 0$, $v_x$ stays constant.

• Opposite side along vertical gives $v_{y0} = v_0 \sin\theta$. Subject to downward acceleration $g$.

2. Position Equations at Time $t$

Expand

Kinematic equations of motion along $X$ and $Y$ axes:

$x(t) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t$
$y(t) = y_0 + v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$

Derivation: From Newton's Second Law with constant acceleration ($a_x = 0, a_y = -g$):

• $x = x_0 + v_{x0}t + \frac{1}{2}a_x t^2 \implies x(t) = v_0\cos\theta \cdot t$ (assuming $x_0 = 0$).

• $y = y_0 + v_{y0}t + \frac{1}{2}a_y t^2 \implies y(t) = y_0 + v_0\sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.

3. Time of Flight & Maximum Height

Expand

For ground-to-ground ($y_0 = 0$):

$T = \frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$   |   $H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$

Derivation:

• Flight Time ($T$): Set $y(T) = 0$ in position equation $v_0\sin\theta T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 = 0 \implies T(v_0\sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gT) = 0$. Rejecting $T=0$, $T = \frac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g}$.

• Max Height ($H$): At peak, vertical velocity $v_y = 0 \implies v_y^2 = v_{y0}^2 - 2gH = 0 \implies H = \frac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$.

4. Horizontal Range ($R$)

Expand

Maximum horizontal displacement for $y_0 = 0$:

$R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$

Derivation: Substitute total flight time $T$ into horizontal position equation $x(T)$:

• $R = (v_0 \cos\theta) \cdot \left(\frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}\right) = \frac{v_0^2 (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)}{g}$.

• Using trigonometric identity $2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin(2\theta)$, we get $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$. Maximum range occurs at $\theta = 45^\circ$ where $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$.

Assumptions & Physical Limitations

  • Uniform Gravity: Gravitational acceleration $g$ is assumed constant in magnitude and direction ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ downwards).
  • Zero Air Resistance: Drag forces ($F_d = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 C_d A$) and buoyant forces are ignored in this ideal model.
  • Flat Earth Approximation: Curvature of the Earth and Coriolis acceleration are neglected over short projectile ranges.
  • Point Mass: The projectile is treated as an idealized point mass without rotational inertia or aerodynamic lift (Magnus effect).
